In Conversation with Sathyaraj Natarajan

Writing Across Time, Emotion, and Expanding Worlds.

Q: Your writing traverses vast cinematic landscapes—what draws you to these expanding worlds?

A: "For me, world-building isn't just about geography or lore; it's about the emotional resonance of the spaces the characters inhabit. When a world expands, so does the capacity for deeper, more complex human emotions."

Q: How do time and history play a role in your storytelling?

A: "History is the anchor. Whether we are in a futuristic setting or an ancient empire, the human condition remains remarkably constant. Time is just the canvas we use to paint those enduring struggles."

Q: What do you hope readers take away from your work?

A: "I want them to feel like they've lived another life. If a story can make you forget your own reality, even for a moment, and adopt the profound emotions of someone else, then I've done my job."
